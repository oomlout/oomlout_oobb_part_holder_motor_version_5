TT MOTOR MOUNT FIT TEST

Print 3dpr.stl once. It contains A, B, C and one shared lower saddle (nut_bar).
A = 0.10 mm per side. B = 0.20 mm per side, used in the current holder.
C = 0.40 mm per side. Shaft clearance and the screw pattern are identical.
Use the material, layer height and orientation planned for the final holder.

1. Set each seat pocket-up. With the leads toward the open end, place the motor
   with its flat output face down and its small off-centre locating pin up.
   Try B first. It should reach the face by hand, sit level and lift out freely.
   Try A and C and record the smallest seat that does this without rocking.
2. Slip two M3 x 30 countersunk machine screws through the seat and gearbox.
   Both should pass by hand with the shaft centred. Binding on both screws
   suggests a different hole pattern. Measure the shaft-to-hole setback and
   hole spacing before changing the case allowance.
3. Slide the common saddle over the motor from the pin side. Check that the
   locating pin enters its recess, the opposite shaft clears the ring and the
   can clears the rear crossbar. Both saddle edges should land on the seat.
   The saddle uses B clearance. If it alone binds, record the contact point.
4. Seat two ordinary M3 hex nuts in the saddle's outer pockets. Start both
   screws, then snug them alternately. Faces should close by hand before
   tightening. Check the nuts resist turning and screw tips stay recessed.
   Check for side movement and compare shaft drag before and after tightening.
   Release and reseat once. Do not use the screws to pull a binding case home.
5. On the four-piece holder repeat the test with the real leads routed out of
   the open end. Check motor retention, housing distortion, bearing rotation
   and accessibility once fixed to the intended OOBB plate.
